# Twelve-plot auxiliary field: production works; midnight delivery fails

2026-09-27. The synthetic native-engine exercise planted and watered all
12 tomatoes, harvested all48 possible units over their finite lifetime,
and fit two workers into hours3–23 from the worst shed-access spawn.
No fertilizer or existing workers were used. This is mechanical evidence,
not a game win or financial return.

All nine recorded baseline schedules finish their relevant ordinary hires
by hour1. Financing is also plausible in the diagnosed tomato-heavy loss:
a day15 start costs8,404 coins including land, seeds and the entire extra
wage bill; subtracting those costs from its full factual baseline cash
trajectory leaves a minimum5,734 without any added crop revenue credit.
Those future recorded balances are diagnostic inputs, not a policy rule.

However, **no start in the nine-game panel passes all requirements**.
The three games with sufficient visible tomato demand each include
harvest midnights with fewer than12 free shed slots. For the liminhai
day15 start, the four midnight headrooms are48,9,20,20: at least three
added units would be clipped under this simple delivery plan. Later starts
also encounter zero-capacity nights. Displacing baseline goods is not a
valid solution.

**Reject the midnight-only delivery design.** Preserve the mechanical and
cash-flow findings. A new design must deliver during the day and sell the
additional stock before the baseline's midnight deposits. Investigate
batch harvests at ages9 and11, water at age10, and an explicit return/drop
trip; tomatoes allow one skipped watering day but never two consecutive
unwatered days. No candidate or full games were run, and main.py is c68.
